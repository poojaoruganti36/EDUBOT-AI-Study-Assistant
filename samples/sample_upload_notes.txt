Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria make their own food using sunlight.

Key requirements:
- Sunlight provides energy.
- Chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light.
- Carbon dioxide enters through stomata in leaves.
- Water is absorbed by roots and transported through the plant.

Main equation:
6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2

Important ideas:
- Glucose is the main food produced.
- Oxygen is released as a by-product.
- The process mainly happens in leaf cells.

There are two major stages:
1. Light-dependent reactions
These happen in the thylakoid membranes. Sunlight energy is captured and used to produce ATP and NADPH. Water molecules are split, which releases oxygen.

2. Calvin cycle
This happens in the stroma of the chloroplast. Carbon dioxide is used along with ATP and NADPH to form glucose.

Why photosynthesis matters:
- It provides food for plants and, directly or indirectly, for nearly all living organisms.
- It releases oxygen into the atmosphere.
- It helps maintain the balance of carbon dioxide and oxygen on Earth.

Common student confusion:
- Plants do respire as well as photosynthesize.
- Photosynthesis mostly occurs during daylight because it depends on light.
- Chlorophyll does not create sunlight; it captures light energy.
